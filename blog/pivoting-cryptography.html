Pivoting to Cryptography With Purpose

March 2025 • Reflection

When I first entered the field of cyber security my focus was largely on systems hardening and offensive techniques such as penetration testing and capture‑the‑flag competitions. Over time, however, I realised that to build truly resilient systems I needed to understand the theoretical foundations that underpin them. This realisation led me to pivot toward cryptography, a discipline that blends mathematics, computer science and security into a coherent whole.

Modern cryptography emphasises precise security definitions and formal models that allow us to reason rigorously about what it means for a scheme to be secure. Unlike ad‑hoc obfuscation, cryptographic constructions are accompanied by proofs that reduce their security to hard mathematical problems such as factoring or discrete logarithms. This perspective demanded that I deepen my knowledge of number theory, modular arithmetic and finite fields – areas of mathematics that I previously only encountered superficially.

At its core, cryptography is about enabling secure communication in the presence of adversaries. As the GeeksforGeeks introduction explains, it develops protocols that prevent malicious parties from learning information that should remain private and it embodies principles such as confidentiality, integrity, authentication and non‑repudiation. These principles are not abstract ideals; they guide the design of encryption schemes, signature algorithms and key‑exchange protocols used every day.

Embarking on this journey has been both challenging and rewarding. Grappling with proofs of security has taught me patience and precision, while building toy implementations of ciphers has kept me grounded in practice. By embracing the rigour of cryptography I hope to contribute to systems that are not only functional but provably secure, bridging the gap between theory and application.
