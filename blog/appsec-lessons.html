Lessons from the Security Innovation AppSec Challenge

June 2024 • Capture‑the‑Flag

In June 2024 I competed in the Security Innovation AppSec Challenge, a capture‑the‑flag competition designed to test and expand participants’ knowledge of web application security. Armed with tools like Burp Suite, Hashcat and SQLmap, the challenge provided a safe environment to exploit deliberately vulnerable applications and practise responsible disclosure. Although the event was gamified, the vulnerabilities and attack vectors were very real.

The contest focused on a broad spectrum of weaknesses including SQL injection, cross‑site scripting (XSS), broken access control and even cryptographic flaws. Tackling these issues required a mix of automated scanners and manual inspection. For example, automated payloads quickly identified reflected and stored XSS, but crafting custom SQL injection strings or bypassing access controls demanded patience and creativity. It was a reminder that while scanners are invaluable, human intuition remains essential when probing an application’s edges.

Beyond the technical skills, the most valuable lesson was the importance of secure coding practices. Many of the exploited vulnerabilities stemmed from missing input validation, improper sanitisation or insecure defaults. By repeatedly seeing how a small oversight could lead to a severe compromise, I developed a greater appreciation for defence in depth. The challenge also reinforced the need for continuous learning; new attack techniques emerge constantly, and staying current is critical for both attackers and defenders.

Overall, participating in the AppSec Challenge deepened my understanding of offensive security and sharpened my ability to think like an adversary—skills that translate directly into more robust defensive solutions. I left inspired to incorporate these insights into my research and teaching, and eager to return for future competitions.
