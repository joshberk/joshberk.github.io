About Me

I'm currently pursuing a Doctor of Philosophy in Information Technology at the University of Cincinnati, with an expected graduation in August 2027. My research interests lie at the intersection of cryptography, secure systems and applied cybersecurity. I enjoy dissecting complex security problems and building practical tools that make the digital world safer. Prior to starting my doctoral studies, I earned my Master of Science in Information Technology from the same institution in August 2024, where I explored advanced topics in network security and cryptanalysis.

My professional journey spans both industry and academia. I interned as a security engineer at Intuit Inc. in San Diego, California, where I integrated and streamlined security repositories for red‑teaming campaigns and implemented automated compliance checks that reduced manual effort significantly. As an adjunct instructor at the University of Cincinnati, I redesigned the IT 2030C course, introduced hands‑on lab components and facilitated projects for nearly 50 students. These experiences sharpened my ability to communicate complex concepts and work collaboratively across disciplines.

Beyond academia, I have defended critical infrastructures as a security operations analyst for Virtual Infosec Africa, where I monitored networks to achieve a 95 percent detection rate and reduced incident response times for the Bank of Ghana. I also enjoy competing in capture‑the‑flag events and bug bounty programs, having solved numerous challenges on platforms like HackerOne and MetaCTF and earning a hall of fame recognition from several organisations.

I’m passionate about sharing knowledge and building community. As a mentor with the OWASP Cincinnati chapter and an AWS Community Builder, I’ve guided aspiring security professionals and contributed documentation to open‑source projects. Looking ahead, I aspire to bridge cutting‑edge research and industry practice as an applied scientist, advancing cryptographic tools that are both theoretically sound and practically deployable.
